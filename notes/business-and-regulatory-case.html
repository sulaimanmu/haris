Business and regulatory case

Value proposition and initial user

Haris helps a bank's AI/security platform owner constrain a single staff case-preparation workflow and give compliance reviewers reconstructable decision evidence. The RM creates a draft for one assigned customer; an independent reviewer controls internal filing. Intended value is less accidental data exposure, fewer unauthorized actions and less work reconstructing why a case was filed. Only the technical controls have been evaluated; operational time savings and willingness to pay are unmeasured.

The first offering would be a small bank-operated reference implementation and integration exercise, not a broad security platform or public SaaS service. A bank's existing IAM, DLP, model platform and SIEM remain relevant. The most plausible sponsor is an AI platform/security lead with a compliance workflow owner. The economic buyer and procurement route have not been confirmed.

Alternatives and defensibility

LiteLLM, Presidio, NeMo and Microsoft tooling already cover much of this technical space. Building another generic proxy has weak defensibility. Haris must earn relevance through customer-specific entitlement integration, exact-content review and practical bilingual evidence that a bank can use. A validated integration pattern, consented benchmark corpus and domain knowledge could become assets. None is an established moat today. See the competitor matrix for overlap rather than claims of vendor inferiority.

Commercial hypothesis and economics

If interviews and a synthetic trial show material value, explore a bank-paid implementation engagement followed by maintenance/support. No price, revenue forecast, total-addressable-market number, pilot commitment or funding source is asserted. A price hypothesis should follow evidence about integration cost, security assurance, service support and buyer budget.

The prototype needs one technical founder and an existing computer. Runtime does not require paid API access. A model download and local compute have real storage/energy costs; “no paid service” does not mean costless operation. The commercial cost stack would include engineering, independent security testing, legal/regulatory advice, bank procurement, infrastructure, support and incident response. Do not commit to an enterprise SLA as a solo founder before arranging operational support.

Development plan for one founder

Already implemented: bounded workflow, synthetic data, two tenants, role/customer authorization, local inference, pattern redaction, independent approval, evidence export and automated tests.

Proposed accelerator milestones, contingent on access and feedback:

  1. Validate the current workflow and buyer with target users, using synthetic tasks and recording consented observations. Decide whether the problem is worth continuing.
  2. Replace weak detection components where needed and expand held-out English/Arabic cases. Keep approval and access controls independent of detector output.
  3. Develop one approved bank-identity/entitlement adapter in an isolated test environment. Add external evidence anchoring and define retention with bank owners.
  4. Conduct an independently reviewed synthetic trial with agreed acceptance criteria and document unresolved failure modes before considering real-data use.

The immediate application target is before 1 October 2026, with a preferred send date of 28 September to leave time for delivery problems. The official brief provides no cutoff hour. MSc commitments and UK location should be stated candidly; attendance arrangements need agreement with the program.

Regulatory perimeter

This is an individual SIAP cybersecurity initiative. It is not a request to provide regulated financial services, obtain a licence, enter the regulatory sandbox, launch electronic payments, wallets, gateways or open banking. The demo neither handles real customer data nor connects to a financial institution. The separate Wolooj sandbox framework must not be represented as the application process for SIAP.

The SIAP brief expressly separates participation from regulatory approval, licensing, sandbox admission, funding and adoption. Haris makes no such claims.

Future deployment by a regulated entity remains subject to that entity's requirements and assessment. CORF, chapter 4, 7.1.1.2 (p.268), describes the approval process for adopting emerging technology; 6.1.1.2–4 (p.265) addresses significant IT third-party engagement and due diligence; cloud arrangements introduce additional requirements under 7.2.1 (p.270). Local hosting alone does not remove these obligations. This is a source-grounded engineering interpretation, not a licensing opinion.

Key risks and stopping rules

No demand: stop or pivot if bank users cannot identify a recurring problem the reference workflow improves. Commodity features: integrate existing components when that produces better assurance. Data protection: remain synthetic until appropriate governance and approvals exist. Model error: preserve human review and do not expand to financial decisions. Solo operations: avoid production uptime commitments without capacity and ownership. Security evaluation: broaden to held-out attacks and representative Arabic; do not extrapolate the reported fixed-case rates to bank traffic.
